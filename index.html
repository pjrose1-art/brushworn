The Brushworn Standard

Don't buy anything from anyone. We'll provide a short list of things we own and use, so you know what you're getting.

Most of what can go wrong is preventable and predictable. Heat, cold, busted boot leather, cut pads, cheatgrass awns, icy water, leaky waders, a long drive home. None of it is in the manual that came with your dog or your gear.

Brushworn is built to fill that gap: field health for dogs that work, and the gear that keeps your trip or training running smoothly. If it hasn't been carried, soaked, frozen, or bled on by us, it doesn't go on the list. Categories will come and go. The standard remains.

What's coming from Brushworn

Kits, protocol cards, and a short list of gear for the trips & training that ask the most of a dog.

  • Field kits

    Season and trip-specific dog first aid

    Not just a generic bag. Kits assembled for the conditions you're actually in (or headed to). Heat, late-season cold water, western scree, all with contents chosen alongside a sporting dog veterinarian. Kits come in blaze for upland or camo and khaki for the waterfowl blind.

  • Protocol cards

    What to do, and the order to do it in

    A waterproof card in every kit, keyed to that kit's contents and laid out for cold, shaking hands. Includes the triggers that indicate it's time to stop and drive to a vet now, and the info that will help you get your dog there safely.

  • Refills

    Restocked before it expires

    Supplies age out and get used up. We'll tell you when yours are due and make replacing them easy with one click.

  • Curated gear

    Short lists, lots of miles

    Kennels, carry, water, warmth, recovery. A tight user-curated selection from other makers, added one piece at a time as the gear earns its place.

Who's behind it

Built by someone who runs dogs and hunts hard.

Field health and gear for dogs that work, and the people who run them.

I'm an upland and waterfowl hunter, blood tracker, and a NAVHDA judge based out of Maine. I've spent a lot of days working out what actually keeps a dog going through grouse cover, prairie grass, oak ridges and January salt water.

Brushworn started because the answers and gear that matter most on a hard day are scattered across forums, phone calls, and hard-earned experience. I believe they should be in one place, curated, written down, and co-signed by the people that know it.
