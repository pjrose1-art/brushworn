The Brushworn Standard

If it hasn't been carried, soaked, frozen, or bled on by us, it isn't Brushworn.

A lot of what can go wrong is preventable and predictable. Heat, cold, busted boot leather, cut pads, cheatgrass awns, icy water, leaky waders, a long drive out of the woods. None of it is in the manual that came with your dog or your gear.

Brushworn makes season and terrain-specific field health kits for dogs that work in punishing conditions, and the gear that keeps a trip or a training day running. Categories will come and go. The standard remains.

Who's behind it

Built by people who run dogs.

Most of what we know about keeping a dog going, we learned from the dogs. Grouse and woodcock cover in Maine, prairie grass out west, blood-tracking deer on oak ridges, and more cold mornings on salt water than we can count.

In addition to hunting, I judge NAVHDA tests, which means a lot of days watching other people's dogs work and noticing what goes wrong in the field, be it with the dog or gear.

The medical side comes from Charles Ellithorpe, DVM, a sporting dog veterinarian who has owned and trained hunting dogs for nearly 40 years. He lives in Maine and hunts the same covers we do. Beyond that, he's hunted big game across the western states, Alaska and British Columbia. He co-authors our field guides and helps build and provision our field kits. Nothing clinical goes out without his eyes on it.

Brushworn exists because the answers that matter on a hard day, and the gear that gets you through them, are scattered across forum threads, phone calls, and mistakes you only make once. We'd rather they were in one place.

See you in the woods.
P.J. & Charles
